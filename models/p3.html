What happens when a PID controller samples too slowly?

A digital PID reads the error only once every Ts seconds and holds the same control signal until the next sample. Increase the sampling time, compare the curves and try anti-windup when the actuator hits its limit.

          Understanding digital PID

          What to look for in the plots.

          Sampling is not continuous feedback

          The loop cannot respond to changes between samples. At each tick it reads y[k], computes e[k] and holds u[k] until the next tick. A long Ts can turn a well-tuned controller into an oscillating one.

          Integral and derivative both depend on Ts

          Integral area is approximated by adding e[k]Ts. The derivative is (e[k] − e[k−1])/Ts. Simply changing Ts without using it in those terms changes the controller.

          Why anti-windup matters

          With a saturated actuator, a large integral may keep demanding impossible control after the error changes sign. Conditional integration skips an update only when it would push the command farther past the active limit.

          Why the actuator trace is a staircase

          The controller executes at discrete times, but the physical plant is continuous. RK4 integrates the plant between controller updates under a fixed (zero-order-held) input.

          Note: this P3 uses backward difference derivative-on-error, exactly as in the homework. P1/P2 use a filtered continuous derivative. Real sensors usually need derivative filtering and noise handling.
